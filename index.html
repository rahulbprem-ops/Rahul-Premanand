Case study: making L.O.T.U.S. hold

Team ASTRA is a first-year design team in Virginia Tech's Archimedes society. For NASA's 2027 Micro-g NExT challenge we proposed L.O.T.U.S., the Lunar Optical Telescopic Unit System. We submitted the proposal in October 2026, and NASA's selections are expected in December. The prototype hasn't been built yet, so every number below comes from design calculations.

My roleStructures · Concept · Test
  • 01
    Wrote the full stress analysis on my ownI hand-calculated four load cases with free-body diagrams, tracked assumptions and cited equations. The methods were bending, combined bending and torsion with von Mises, impact loading and Euler buckling, all checked against NASA's 2.0 factor of safety.
  • 02
    Proposed the cascading extension systemSix nested triangular sections with V-groove pulleys and Kevlar line. One crank raises every section together, so no section can drop on its own.
  • 03
    Shaped the design conceptI contributed to the design direction of the stowable tripod and mast.
  • 04
    Planned verification with the head of testingWe tied the analysis to the proposed test plan: function, cam-lock, dust, stability and user-range tests to run before NASA's Neutral Buoyancy Lab.

Each 20 in section rises 18 in out of the one below it. All five moving sections are tied to one spool, so 18 in of string on the crank moves the top 90 in.

Stress analysis · Factor of safety by load caseRequirement: FoS ≥ 2.0
Key result

The analysis changed the design

A PETG spool axle came out at FoS 1.90, below NASA's limit. I moved it to 304 steel, which took it to 25.6.

Judgment

I flagged the risk past the requirement

A 4 ft drop in Earth gravity gives FoS ≈ 1.08 on mast section 1. NASA didn't require this check, but it went into the hazard analysis and the handling rules.

Verification

Actuation stays inside NASA's limits

By my calculations, raising the stand takes ≤ 6.56 lb against a 20 lb limit, and turning the crank takes 24.6 in·lb against a 30 in·lb limit. Both figures double the friction allowance to stay conservative.
